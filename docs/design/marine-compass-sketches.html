Marine compass, five ways

Five compass faces drawn in the Classic Steel idiom — black-metal bezel, anthracite dial, LCD window, red pointer — so the family reads with the existing widget-gauge-steel RPM face. Every dial below runs off one heading, so you can swing them together and see which one still reads at a glance. B is built and shipped as the Steel Compass widget; the other four stay here as the alternatives it was picked over.

What each one costs

Already in the box

  • src/assets/steelseries-min.js ships a Compass and a WindDirection gauge — A and B are close to what those draw out of the box.
  • widget-gauge-ng-compass already does rotating-card and faceplate modes on canvas-gauges, so the behaviour (card vs needle, cardinal letters vs numbers) is settled — this is a skin, not new physics.
  • C, D and E want overlays neither library draws; those are our own SVG on top of, or instead of, the canvas.

Paths it would read

  • navigation.headingMagnetic — the card or needle, all five.
  • navigation.courseOverGroundTrue — the amber chevron on C.
  • environment.wind.angleApparent — the cyan barb on C, already the negative-to-port path the compass widget special-cases.
  • navigation.courseGreatCircle.nextPoint.bearingTrue — waypoint diamond on C.
  • steering.autopilot.target.headingMagnetic and steering.rudderAngle — the bug and rudder strip on D.

Things to decide with the sketch

  • Card or needle. A rotating card matches the binnacle on the boat; a needle matches the RPM gauge on the same dashboard. They can't both be the default.
  • Magnetic or true, and whether the dial says which — every face here writes °M next to the number for that reason.
  • Small-tile legibility: hit 1×1 above. That is the Freeboard plotter tile, and it's where the full rose stops working.
  • Whether the LCD window earns its space, or the number belongs on the dial face.
